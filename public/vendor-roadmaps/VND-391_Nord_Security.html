What is a VPN with post-quantum encryption?

Post-quantum (PQ) encryption is a cryptographic system built to defend against quantum computer attacks. Unlike traditional encryption, which quantum computers could break in seconds, post-quantum cryptography uses complex mathematical techniques that make encryption resistant to quantum decryption attempts.

Although current VPN encryption algorithms are sufficient enough to protect your sensitive data, the rapid progress of quantum computing could change that fast. That’s why we’ve created a quantum-resistant VPN — to keep your security future-proof.

NordVPN’s post-quantum encryption for stronger security

Protection against quantum threats

One toggle switch to a quantum-safe future. Post-quantum encryption is a proactive way to prevent quantum attacks, even after quantum computers become accessible for consumer use.

Even stronger data security

Hackers want your passwords, banking details, private emails, sensitive data, and everything in between. If quantum computers become mainstream, no data would be safe. But not with PQ encryption, built to resist quantum threats.

Defense against future decryption

Although quantum threats aren't here yet, PQ encryption ensures long-term security. Attackers are already collecting encrypted data to decrypt later with quantum computing. But if your data is PQ encrypted now, even the most powerful quantum computer won’t be able to decrypt it later.

Frequently asked questions

Post-quantum security is a set of cryptographic methods and systems designed to ensure that encryption methods will still be effective if quantum computers become a practical threat. Although quantum devices are still being developed, they could eventually crack existing encryption algorithms that protect our sensitive online data. Modern problems require modern solutions, so this type of encryption uses new, different math to protect your data.

NordVPN has just released an update with a quantum-safe cryptography feature for the NordLynx protocol. While it’s not available on our other protocols like OpenVPN, we are already working on building defenses to protect your data from the potential threats quantum computers might bring in the future.

Ideally, every VPN provider should start thinking about implementing post-quantum encryption because technology evolves relatively fast, and one day, quantum computers could become accessible to anyone, including hackers. However, VPNs don’t need to urgently deploy post-quantum encryption right now, since quantum threats are still mostly theoretical at this point.

Using a VPN with post-quantum encryption could potentially slow down your internet connection, but the impact should generally be minimal. Developers are still fine-tuning post-quantum encryption algorithms, so once optimized, the effect on internet speed should be barely noticeable.

The post-quantum encryption option is now available in the latest version of NordVPN on Windows, Mac, Linux, Android, iOS, and even Android TV and tvOS. Just open the app on your device, go to "Settings," and switch on the "Post-quantum encryption" option — that’s it.
