The massive 5G cellular networks powering millions of smartphones and connected vehicles rely on incredibly complex webs of digital trust between cell towers, devices, and the internet core.

Because a quantum computer could effortlessly shatter the digital locks protecting these high-speed connections, attackers could theoretically instantly reroute or intercept massive amounts of global cellular traffic. As of 3GPP's 5G security specification TS 33.501 (Release 19, V19.5.0, February 2026), the work on subscriber identities is at the recommendation stage: groups such as ATIS and GSMA publish guidance on how operators and 3GPP can prepare, but 3GPP has not standardised any post-quantum protection for subscriber identities in 5G.
