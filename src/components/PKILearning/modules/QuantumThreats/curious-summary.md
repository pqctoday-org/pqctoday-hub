# Quantum Threats — In Simple Terms

## What This Is About

Modern encryption relies on math problems (like factoring massive numbers) that are practically impossible for traditional computers to solve. A Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC) running "Shor's Algorithm" would change that: it could solve the problems behind RSA and ECC far faster than any classical computer. No such machine is publicly known to exist, and expert estimates of when one might arrive range from a few years to a few decades.

## Why It Matters

Government security agencies, including NSA, CISA and the UK NCSC, warn that adversaries may be recording encrypted data today to decrypt it once a powerful enough quantum computer exists ("Harvest Now, Decrypt Later"). Data that must stay secret for many years — health records, state secrets, intellectual property — is therefore at risk now, even though the machine is not.

## The Key Takeaway

Waiting for a quantum computer to appear before reacting would be too late for long-lived secrets. Organizations should identify their most sensitive long-lived data first and plan its migration to NIST's post-quantum standards (FIPS 203, 204, 205) well before a CRQC could exist.

## What's Happening

Several governments have published transition timelines. The U.S. has the most specific ones: CNSA 2.0 for National Security Systems and Executive Order 14412 for federal agencies. Others, such as the UK NCSC and the EU's coordinated roadmap, publish target dates and guidance rather than binding rules for everyone.
