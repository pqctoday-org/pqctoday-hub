### What This Is About

PQC GRC is the governance, risk, and compliance machinery that makes a post-quantum migration measurable and auditable. Where the SOC detects and responds, GRC measures, reports, and governs — turning a multi-year migration into something a board can actually oversee.

### Why It Matters

Quantum risk has no historical incident base rate or reliable date for a cryptanalytically relevant quantum computer. It is also a technology, compliance, strategic, and operational risk. Logging it as "low likelihood, review annually" is acknowledging the risk, not managing it. The fix is to give quantum risk the same structured treatment as any other top-tier risk.

NIST IR 8547 became final in October 2026. For federal high-value assets and high-impact systems, it identifies earlier targets for post-quantum key establishment (31 December 2030) and signatures (31 December 2031), mandated for federal civilian agencies under EO 14412 and OMB M-26-15 (which also directs a proposed FAR contractor rule). NIST plans to deprecate quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography after 2030 and disallow it under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035. Keep scopes separate: NSA CNSA 2.0 FAQ v2.2 governs National Security Systems (2027/2030/2031), EU Member States coordinate under the EU PQC Roadmap v1.1, and DORA/NIS2 impose cyber risk-management duties rather than fixed algorithm mandates. Other organizations must check their own applicable rules. The module recommends quarterly horizon review as a governance practice, not a universal legal requirement. [NIST IR 8547](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final)

### The Key Takeaway

Three instruments do most of the work: a Key Risk Indicator cascade that shows the board, the CISO, and operations the right metrics at the right cadence with thresholds that trigger specific action; a risk-appetite statement that lets the program make trade-offs without escalating every one; and a clean GRC-SOC handoff in which the exception register becomes the SOC's suppression list — so genuine cryptographic drift is not lost in alerts from systems that were legitimately deferred.

### What's Happening

Whether your insurer asks about quantum readiness at renewal is a question for your broker, but a documented, governed, measured migration program is easier to explain to an underwriter than a vague intention to migrate later. The organizations that maintain an evidence dossier can answer the attestation surge after any public quantum event with an export, not a project.
