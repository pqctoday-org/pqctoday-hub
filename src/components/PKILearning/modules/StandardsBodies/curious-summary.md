### What This Is About

The post-quantum ecosystem involves three distinct roles: Standards bodies (like NIST) write the algorithm specifications, Certification bodies (like CMVP) test the products, and Compliance frameworks (like CNSA 2.0) mandate their use in specific sectors.

### Why It Matters

Confusing these roles is a common source of compliance failure. For example, purchasing a product that merely uses a "NIST algorithm" is insufficient if your framework requires a CMVP FIPS 140-3 validation, which itself involves a long certification queue.

### The Key Takeaway

You comply with a framework, get certified against a standard, and the standard is written by the standard body. Multinational teams must track NIST globally, ETSI in the EU, and specific national mandates (like BSI in Germany or OSCCA in China).

### What's Happening

The CMVP FIPS 140-3 certification backlog is a critical bottleneck for organizations requiring validated PQC modules: a few module certificates already list ML-KEM or ML-DSA as approved algorithms, but most do not. Meanwhile, regional bodies like ETSI, BSI, and OSCCA are publishing their own PQC guidance to address jurisdiction-specific requirements.
