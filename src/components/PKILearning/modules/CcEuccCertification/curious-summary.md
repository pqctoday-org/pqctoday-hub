### What This Is About

A closer look at the certificates that matter most in Europe: Common Criteria, the EU's EUCC scheme built on it, and the eIDAS rules for qualified signature and seal devices. It builds on the certification fundamentals module.

### Why It Matters

A Common Criteria grade such as "EAL4+" only means something once you know which extra checks were added. A CC evaluation may support an EUCC certificate or form part of an eIDAS device-assessment chain, while international CCRA recognition remains a separate, conditional question.

### The Key Takeaway

"EAL4+" means nothing until its extra components are named, and EUCC is a legal scheme — not a Protection Profile. Certification engineers must also use the current mandatory CC:2022 errata, not only the base documents.

### What's Happening

As of November 2026, the EU's EUCC scheme (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482, succeeding SOG-IS national certificates across the EU) lists post-quantum algorithms in its applicable ECCG Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms v2 guidance and says lattice-based mechanisms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA) should be combined with a classical mechanism, while US NIAP Policy #33 sets CNSA 2.0 cut-offs starting 1 January 2028 for products evaluated by NIAP or CCRA partners.

The lesson also includes Security Target source-reading cases (reviewed November 2026) and two collapsed reference panels: an invented example of how three post-quantum requirements could be written in a Security Target, and a box on how a laboratory might look at attacks on lattice-based code. No certification scheme or laboratory has reviewed either reference panel.
