### What This Is About

A closer look at the certificates that matter most in Europe: Common Criteria, the EU's EUCC scheme built on it, and the eIDAS rules for qualified signature and seal devices. It builds on the certification fundamentals module.

### Why It Matters

A Common Criteria grade such as "EAL4+" only means something once you know which extra checks were added. A CC evaluation may support an EUCC certificate or form part of an eIDAS device-assessment chain, while international CCRA recognition remains a separate, conditional question.

### The Key Takeaway

"EAL4+" means nothing until its extra components are named, and EUCC is a legal scheme — not a Protection Profile. Certification engineers must also use the current mandatory CC:2022 errata, not only the base documents.

### What's Happening

As of 24 September 2026, the EU's scheme already lists post-quantum algorithms in its approved cryptography guidance, and says lattice-based algorithms should be combined with a classical one.

The lesson also has two panels that stay closed until you open them. One is an invented example of how three post-quantum requirements could be written in a Security Target. The other is a box on how a laboratory might look at attacks on lattice-based code. No certification scheme or laboratory has reviewed either one.
