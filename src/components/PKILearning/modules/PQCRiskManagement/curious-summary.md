### What This Is About

PQC risk management provides a framework to prepare for the possible arrival of a Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC). It involves modeling CRQC timelines as scenarios (no one knows when one will exist) and identifying which cryptographic assets (like RSA and ECC) would be exposed.

### Why It Matters

A CRQC could break widely used public-key algorithms such as RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography. Rating each asset by Likelihood x Impact helps prioritize migration efforts, including HNDL scenarios where ciphertext is captured, protected by quantum-vulnerable public-key key establishment, still needs confidentiality at the assumed arrival time, and secrets could be recovered.

### The Key Takeaway

Use the dates that actually apply to you. If you supply U.S. National Security Systems, CNSA 2.0 (FAQ v2.2) sets them; U.S. federal civilian agencies follow EO 14412 and OMB M-26-15; otherwise use your regulator's and your customers' dates (noting that NIST IR 8547 sets NIST standards status and EU DORA/NIS2 impose cyber risk-management duties rather than fixed PQC dates). The method has four steps: CRQC scenario planning, building a risk register for assets the organization has identified as in scope, visualizing exposure on a 5x5 likelihood-impact heatmap to guide executive decision-making, and checking applicable policy, standards-status and guidance dates alongside the register. The heatmap ranks your assets against each other; it does not measure when a quantum computer will arrive.

### What's Happening

Organizations are incorporating CRQC scenario planning into their enterprise risk frameworks, using likelihood-impact heatmaps to prioritize which cryptographic systems must migrate first based on the HNDL conditions and deadlines that apply to them. Worksheet scores are relative teaching ratings, not measured probabilities or compliance findings.
