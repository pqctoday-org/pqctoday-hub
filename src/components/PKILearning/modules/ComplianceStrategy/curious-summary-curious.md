A few governments have set binding post-quantum dates for specific systems. For example, the U.S. requires it for National Security Systems (CNSA 2.0) and for federal high-impact systems (Executive Order 14412). Most other publications are standards, recommendations or roadmaps, and they don't all agree. Some aim for a full switch to the new math around 2030–2035. Others, such as France's ANSSI, ask for "hybrid mode" first: old and new math running together. ANSSI requires this only inside France's regulated scope and recommends it elsewhere.

A company that works in several countries has to sort out which of these actually apply to it. Missing a binding date can cost government contracts or block a product's certification, and customers increasingly ask about post-quantum plans when they buy.

A successful strategy starts with a map of every cryptographic lock inside a company's infrastructure. Comparing that map with the dates that really apply lets a company test and upgrade its most critical systems first, well before any deadline.
