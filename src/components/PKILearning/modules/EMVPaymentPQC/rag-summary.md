# Financial Services & Payments PQC — RAG Summary

## Module Overview

Advanced-level module (120 min, 8 workshop steps, three learner paths) covering post-quantum cryptography migration across the whole payments and banking estate: the EMV card ecosystem (14.7 billion chip cards at the end of 2024, across Visa, Mastercard, Amex, UnionPay and Discover — the world's largest deployed PKI), retail and card-not-present acceptance, interbank settlement rails, bank key management, open banking APIs and PSD2 Strong Customer Authentication, and the sector regulation setting the pace.

Three paths so no audience is asked for the full 120 minutes: **Cards & Acceptance** (55 min, issuers/acquirers/processors/terminal estates), **Banking & Settlement** (55 min, banks/central banks/financial market infrastructure), **Retail & E-Commerce** (25 min, merchants/PSPs/platforms).

## Key Topics

- **EMV Card Authentication**: SDA (static RSA), DDA (dynamic RSA per-transaction), CDA (combined with application cryptogram). All use RSA-2048 certificate chains vulnerable to quantum computers.
- **Payment Network Comparison**: 5 networks compared by scale, crypto stack, official PQC stance, public evidence and public or clearly labelled tentative plan. Mastercard has published migration guidance and described prototype testing; Visa has published stablecoin and digital-payment readiness guidance; American Express publicly describes an enterprise readiness program. No network-specific roadmap was located for UnionPay or Discover, so the comparator separates their shared EMVCo work from dependency-based inference.
- **Transaction Flows**: Online authorization (ARQC/ARPC symmetric MACs — quantum-safe), offline DDA/CDA (RSA signatures — quantum-vulnerable), contactless, and mobile payment flows.
- **Card Provisioning**: 5-phase process (Chip OS, Pre-Perso, Personalization, Key Injection, Activation) with RSA certificate chain. FN-DSA-512 (666-byte signatures, ~5.1 KB for a three-certificate chain) is smaller than ML-DSA-44 (2,420-byte signatures, ~11.5 KB); the card itself carries only the issuer and ICC certificates, the terminal holding the payment-system CA public key, which is the size argument for constrained card NVM; in its March 2024 position statement EMVCo names no post-quantum algorithm for EMV cards, and the card-side evidence is research (a 2022 IDEMIA prototype, a basic "toy" application profile on an Arm Cortex-M3 derivative, where Dilithium-5 data could not be loaded and most transaction time was the card certificate chain being sent to the terminal; a Banque de France estimate, cited by Mastercard, of 4 to 6 times more chip RAM). FIPS 206 is still in preparation.
- **Tokenization**: TSP architectures (Visa VTS, Mastercard MDES, Amex EST) replacing PANs with tokens. Mobile wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Samsung Pay) with secure element details. Per-transaction cryptograms are AES and quantum-safe; the exposure is in provisioning.
- **E-Commerce, CNP & Retail Checkout**: TLS-borne cardholder data and 3-D Secure 2.x ECDSA challenge signing. The fastest migration path in the estate — hybrid ML-KEM TLS 1.3 is already deployable. PCI SSC lists a separate PCI 3DS Core standard among its standards; this module does not state what it covers or requires. The hybrid TLS scheme was published as RFC 10024 (IETF Standards Track, August 2026); the native ML-KEM TLS scheme is still an Internet-Draft.
- **POS Terminals**: 5 types (Traditional POS, mPOS, SoftPOS, ATM, Unattended) with DUKPT key management. The DUKPT derivation chain is symmetric and quantum-safe; the Key Injection Facility ceremony's RSA-2048 transport of the BDK is the quantum vulnerability. The 3DES-to-AES move for PIN encryption is classical modernisation, not a quantum fix (EMVCo: symmetric choices are driven by classical attack feasibility, and 2-key Triple DES keeps a significant margin against Grover). Device certification: PCI SSC assigns and lists PTS approvals for HSMs and POI devices; whether a PCI operational standard accepts a FIPS certificate in place of a PTS HSM approval is set in that standard’s own requirement text, which this module does not state.
- **Interbank Rails & Settlement**: Swift messaging (FIN / ISO 20022), domestic RTGS, and correspondent banking chains. HNDL exposure is driven by how long data stays sensitive, not by a guessed CRQC date; the Settlement Exposure Modeller applies Mosca's theorem (data shelf life plus migration time versus time to a CRQC, as in the Federal Reserve HNDL paper FEDS 2025-093) with the migration-time term set to zero, so its result is a floor. The correspondent chain cannot be migrated unilaterally, which is why the sector publishes jointly.
- **Banking Key Management & Key Blocks**: A key wrapped together with its usage attributes so it cannot be repurposed — the attribute binding is the security property. The current standard is ANSI X9.143, not the more frequently cited X9 TR-31. X9.143, X9.24-1/-2 and TR-31 are paywalled and are cited as normative-but-unobtainable rather than paraphrased.
- **Sector Regulation**: Nine bodies across six jurisdictions — BIS Project Leap Phase 2 (in which the RSA signature on the Business Application Header of the payment messages was replaced by a CRYSTALS-Dilithium signature, as a technical experiment), G7 Cyber Expert Group, UK CMORG (names ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA), Europol Quantum Safe Financial Forum, FS-ISAC, MAS, DORA, the Nacha Payments Innovation Alliance roadmap (a recommendation, not a mandate: all requests for information and proposals should require vendors to be quantum-safe by 2030), and the EU NIS CG Coordinated Implementation Roadmap. Unlike Government & Defense, none carries a dated _algorithm_ mandate of the CNSA 2.0 kind — but the EU roadmap sets a dated _transition expectation_ (high-risk use cases by end-2030) that reaches EU banks via NIS2/DORA, without naming an algorithm. Otherwise, pressure comes from operational-resilience regulation and HNDL exposure.
- **Open Banking & PSD2 Strong Customer Authentication**: PSD2 Article 97 requires two-factor SCA (knowledge/possession/inherence) and dynamic transaction linking for remote payments. Its RTS (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/389) implements the "dedicated interface" third-party providers use to access accounts (Art. 30), a mandatory fallback if that interface fails (Art. 33), eIDAS qualified certificates (QSealC/QWAC) for AISP/PISP identification (Art. 34), and a channel-encryption requirement worded as "strong and widely recognised encryption techniques" — deliberately technology-neutral, naming no algorithm or TLS version, so PQC migration needs no legislative change.
- **Migration Planning**: 10 payment components mapped by severity and effort. The 2030 and 2035 markers on the timeline are NIST IR 8547's trajectory for NIST standards and federal systems (RSA and elliptic-curve algorithms deprecated after 2030, disallowed under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035), not a payments-sector mandate. PCI PTS HSM v5.0 (announced 18 May 2026) adds "support for post-quantum cryptography considerations"; the PTS listing’s expiry table gives HSM v4.x requirements approval June 2027 and device models April 2033, which are approval-expiry dates and not post-quantum deadlines. The public P2PE pages (program page and v3.2 announcement) do not mention post-quantum cryptography. PKCS#11 v3.2 (OASIS Standard, 3 June 2026) defines ML-KEM key-encapsulation mechanisms. Critical path: HSM key wrapping → KIF key injection → card personalization → offline auth. How long the migration takes depends on card and terminal replacement cycles; this module assumes a 3-5 year card cycle as an estimate, not a sourced figure, and derives no minimum timeline from it.
