Identity and Access Management (IAM) systems decide who can access an organization's services and data. They combine several mechanisms, including passwords, symmetric authenticators, public-key signatures, key exchange, and policy checks.

The quantum risk depends on which mechanism is used. A sufficiently capable quantum computer could break widely used RSA and elliptic-curve signatures, enabling forged signed tokens or certificates. It could also break classical public-key key exchange, exposing recorded TLS traffic. These are different risks: forging an identity token is not the same as decrypting stored data. Symmetric mechanisms such as HMAC and AES need separate analysis and are not made equivalent to RSA or elliptic-curve cryptography. A migration plan should inventory protocols, token lifetimes, trust relationships, product support, and the data or access each mechanism protects.
