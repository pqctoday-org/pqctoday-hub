# IoT & Embedded Device PQC

## Overview

This advanced Applications module (LM-074, 90 minutes) covers post-quantum cryptography for constrained IoT and embedded devices. It was split on 2026-10-01 from the former IoT/OT and Energy & Utilities modules: industrial control systems (SCADA, PLCs, IEC 62443 zones and conduits, sector deep dives) moved to LM-075 _OT & Industrial Control Systems PQC_; vehicle networks to _Automotive PQC_; boot-time verification to _Secure Boot PQC_. Smart-meter key management and RF-mesh airtime moved in from Energy & Utilities and were generalised to device fleets and LPWAN.

Numbers in the module are of three kinds and are labelled that way: exact sizes from the standards (FIPS 203 Table 3, FIPS 204 Table 2, RFC 8554, RFC 8391, Falcon v1.2); Cortex-M4 benchmarks with a named source (pqm4 benchmarks.md at commit 90bfb63 of 2025-05-22, pqm4 at 33de42d of 2021-09-06 for FrodoKEM, Campos et al. IACR ePrint 2020/470 for LMS/XMSS, the Emill P256-Cortex-M4 library for ECDSA/ECDH); and model estimates computed from those.

## Learn sections

1. **Why IoT is different: device classes** — RFC 7228 Classes 0–2 (≪10 KiB / ~10 KiB / ~50 KiB RAM) and Classes 3–4 from draft-ietf-iotops-7228bis (~100 KiB; ~300–1000 KiB). An IEEE 802.15.4 frame is 127 bytes with about 80 bytes of payload once link security is on. Lifetimes, radio cost, energy. Ascon (NIST SP 800-232, final August 2025) for lightweight symmetric crypto.
2. **Algorithm selection: verify vs sign** — devices mostly verify. Stack RAM on Cortex-M4 (pqm4): ML-KEM-768 decapsulation 2,844 B in the stack-optimised build vs 6,452 B speed-optimised; ML-DSA-44 verify 2,712 B / sign 5,080 B (stack build), verify 8,912 B in the speed build; FN-DSA-512 verify 2,976 B but sign 41,952 B with ~104 KB of code; LMS H10/W4 verify 1,044 B (reference code); FrodoKEM-640 decapsulation ~72 KB. FIPS 206 is not yet published; FN-DSA-512 signatures are 666 B in Falcon v1.2's padded format.
3. **Firmware and update signing (SUIT, COSE)** — LMS/HSS (60 B key, 2,512 B signature as HSS L=1), XMSS (68 B / 2,500 B), ML-DSA (stateless). Stateful counters must never roll back. Verification cost on Cortex-M4, fastest build of each: FN-DSA-512 0.40 M < ECDSA P-256 0.98 M < ML-DSA-44 1.42 M < ML-DSA-65 2.42 M < LMS 2.66 M < ML-DSA-87 4.19 M < XMSS 13.07 M cycles — LMS is not the fastest verifier. CNSA 2.0 accepts only ML-DSA-87 or LMS/XMSS for national-security-system firmware (prefer from 2025, exclusive by 2030). SUIT = RFC 9019 (architecture) + RFC 9124 (information model) + draft-ietf-suit-manifest (CBOR format). COSE: HSS-LMS in RFC 8778, ML-DSA in RFC 9964; SLH-DSA and FN-DSA still drafts.
4. **Constrained protocols** — CoAP + DTLS 1.3 (RFC 7252, RFC 9147, RFC 7925 and the TLS 1.3 IoT profile draft); EDHOC (RFC 9528) + OSCORE (RFC 8613) with PQ suites in draft-ietf-lake-pqsuites; MQTT 5 (maximum packet 268,435,455 B over TCP/TLS); LwM2M; Matter (PASE with SPAKE2+, DAC → PAI → PAA attestation, CASE); Bluetooth Mesh (P-256 provisioning in PB-ADV segments, certificate-based provisioning in Mesh Protocol 1.1); LoRaWAN 1.1 (symmetric AES-128 air interface — the quantum exposure is backend TLS and root-key provisioning). Model: classical DTLS 1.3 ≈ 1,374 B; ML-KEM-768 + ML-DSA-44 ≈ 13,146 B in 13 IPv6 datagrams and 171 802.15.4 frames; EDHOC with credentials by reference ≈ 216 B classical, ≈ 7,141 B with the same PQC algorithms.
5. **Certificates and device identity** — each certificate holds the subject key and the issuer's signature (RFC 5280); TLS/DTLS sends leaf + intermediate and omits the root (RFC 8446 §4.4.2). Model chain bytes sent: ECDSA 856 B, RSA-2048 1,624 B, FN-DSA-512 3,726 B, LMS 5,744 B, ML-DSA-44 8,064 B, ML-DSA-65 11,122 B, ML-DSA-87 15,038 B. Mitigations: RFC 8879 compression (only the structured bytes compress), C509 (draft-ietf-cose-cbor-encoded-cert), RFC 7250 raw public keys, PSK resumption, Merkle Tree Certificates (draft-ietf-plants-merkle-tree-certs: 32-byte hashes per tree level; needs current trusted tree heads at the relying party). Identity: IEEE 802.1AR IDevID (NIST SP 1800-36B), FIDO Device Onboard 1.1, EST-coaps (RFC 9148).
6. **Fleet key management and LPWAN** — fleet rotation is limited by the head-end HSM more often than by the radio; harvest-now-decrypt-later risk for metering data; DLMS/COSEM Suite 0 (symmetric only), Suite 1 (P-256), Suite 2 (P-384); Wi-SUN FAN 1.1 (FSK 50–300 kbit/s, OFDM up to 2.4 Mbit/s), LoRaWAN (51–222 B per uplink in EU868, 1% duty cycle), NB-IoT (Cat-NB1 ≈ 26 kbit/s downlink, 62.5 kbit/s uplink). A PQC signature is a few percent of a firmware update (ML-DSA-87: 2.9% of a 150 KiB image); unicast vs multicast decides whether a cell can be updated.
7. **Hardware support** — secure elements with on-chip PQC (Infineon TEGRION with Common Criteria EAL6 for an ML-KEM implementation; Samsung S3SSE2A, among the first ANSSI PQC certifications in 2025; STMicroelectronics ST54M); ECC-only parts such as Microchip ATECC608B cannot offload PQC; TrustZone-M is isolation on the same core, not a secure element; TCG TPM 2.0 Library v1.85 adds ML-KEM and ML-DSA; PSA Certified / SESIP / EN 17927 assurance.
8. **Hybrid on constrained hardware** — X25519MLKEM768 stays secret if either component is unbroken; the classical half adds 32 bytes each way, so hybrid does not double the cost. Gateway-mediated (Class 0–1) vs native hybrid.
9. **IoT security regulations** — EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847: reporting from 11 September 2026, essential requirements from 11 December 2027); ETSI EN 303 645; RED Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 with EN 18031-1/-2/-3 (applies from 1 August 2025; cited by Implementing Decision (EU) 2025/138); US Cyber Trust Mark (FCC 24-26); UK PSTI Regulations 2023 (SI 2023/1007); NIST IR 8259 (withdrawn 2026-04-20, superseded by 8259r1) and NIST IR 8259A. None names a PQC algorithm.

## Workshop Steps

1. **Algorithm Explorer** (`constrained-algorithm`) — choose a device class (0–4), a role (verify, sign, key establishment) and a stack- or speed-optimised build; each algorithm's peak RAM is its Cortex-M4 benchmark stack plus the keys/ciphertext/signature it holds, with cycles, code size and the benchmark source. The summary of what fits is generated from the same model.
2. **Firmware Signing** (`firmware-signing`) — hash a firmware image, build a SUIT-style manifest and sign it for real in the browser's SoftHSM with ML-DSA-44/65/87 or LMS (HSS L=1, H10/W4, with the token's remaining-signature counter); a one-bit-tampered manifest is shown to fail. XMSS, FN-DSA-512 and ECDSA are size-only and labelled. Shows the COSE_Sign1 layout (COSE alg −46 HSS-LMS, −48/−49/−50 ML-DSA), CNSA 2.0 status, update overhead on the device's link, and a verification-cost ranking. Known-answer tests: NIST ACVP ML-DSA-44 and ML-DSA-87 sigVer, and RFC 8554 Appendix F Test Cases 1–2 through the same CKM_HSS verifier (with a tampered-signature negative check).
3. **Constrained Handshake** (`constrained-handshake`) — DTLS 1.3 vs EDHOC message sizes for a chosen KEM (X25519, ML-KEM-512/768, X25519MLKEM768) and signature (ECDSA, ML-DSA-44/65, FN-DSA-512), with IPv6 datagrams and 802.15.4 frame counts; a BLE Mesh / Matter provisioning view counts PB-ADV segments for PQC keys and sizes the DAC + PAI chain.
4. **Certificate Chain** (`cert-chain`) — pick root, intermediate and leaf algorithms; certificates carry the issuer's signature; the root is stored, not sent; delivery modes: full chain, RFC 8879 compression, C509, raw public key, Merkle Tree Certificate (adjustable tree depth), PSK resumption — each with its caveat — against a device RAM budget.
5. **Fleet Key Manager** (`fleet-key-manager`) — fleet type (smart meters, asset trackers, building sensors), fleet size, devices per cell, link (NB-IoT, G3-PLC, Wi-SUN, LTE-M, LoRaWAN), today's key establishment (DLMS Suite 0/1/2), ML-KEM parameter set, rotation frequency and HSM rating; outputs per-cell network time, HSM time, the bottleneck, share of the rotation period and yearly traffic. Known-answer tests: NIST ACVP ML-KEM-768 decapsulation, NIST CAVP AES-256-GCM decryption, and an ECDH P-256 round-trip for the classical baseline.
6. **LPWAN Airtime** (`lpwan-airtime`) — deliver a signed firmware image over Wi-SUN FAN 1.1 (FSK or OFDM, with mesh hops), NB-IoT or LoRaWAN (frame-by-frame time on air and a 1% duty cycle), unicast or multicast, and compare the result with the same update signed with ECDSA.
