Right now, your bank accounts, private messages and company secrets are protected by mathematical locks. Used and set up correctly, no one can practically pick them today.

Scientists are working to build large "quantum computers". These machines use the rules of quantum physics to solve a few specific kinds of math problems much faster than any ordinary computer. If someone builds one that is big and reliable enough, it could break the locks that protect most internet connections and digital signatures today. No such machine exists in public yet, and no one knows when, or whether, one will.

"Post-quantum cryptography" (PQC) is the upgrade: new mathematical locks that experts currently believe resist both ordinary and quantum computers. The first three NIST standards for them were finalised in 2024. Fitting them into websites, apps, devices and certifications is the work still under way.
