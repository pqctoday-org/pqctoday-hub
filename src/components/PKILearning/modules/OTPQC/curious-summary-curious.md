The computers that run power grids, water plants, railways and factories stay in service for decades. They only accept software updates and commands that carry a valid digital signature — a kind of tamper-proof seal from the manufacturer or the control room.

A powerful future quantum computer could fake those seals. That is why operators are already moving the keys that sign updates and commands to new quantum-safe schemes, and protecting the remote connections into their plants, while leaving the ultra-fast safety signals — which already use quantum-safe codes — exactly as they are.

Attackers usually get in through the company's office network, through remote connections such as vendor links and internet-connected sensors, or by being on site, where most plant equipment accepts commands from anyone. Each route needs its own fix.
