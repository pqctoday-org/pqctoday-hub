# Researcher Quantum Impact

## Overview

The Researcher Quantum Impact module addresses the unique cryptographic challenges faced by academic researchers, data scientists, and interdisciplinary scientists whose work involves long-lived sensitive datasets, collaborative data sharing, federated infrastructure, and emerging PQC research directions. It covers six research-specific concerns — long-lived confidential data exposure, publication and peer-review integrity, research infrastructure, current PQC research directions, cross-disciplinary PQC applications, and research funding — in a four-step workshop (Why It Matters, What to Learn, How to Act, and a nine-item Research Readiness self-assessment). Content is calibrated for researchers who must think about both protecting their own data and contributing to or consuming PQC knowledge.

## Key Concepts

- **Long-Lived Research Data Exposure (HNDL)** — participant-linked genomic and biomedical data can need confidentiality for decades (patient privacy, research embargoes, competitive advantage). For clinical trial records, FDA 21 CFR Part 11 governs electronic records and electronic signatures; the retention period comes from the applicable predicate rule (for IND investigators, 21 CFR 312.62(c): 2 years after the marketing application is approved, or 2 years after the investigation is discontinued and FDA is notified), plus sponsor, jurisdiction and contract terms. EU Regulation 536/2014 (Art. 58), for example, requires the clinical trial master file to be archived for at least 25 years after the trial ends. Determine the period before sizing HNDL exposure. Grant archives can also hold proprietary pre-publication findings for many years. Any data encrypted today with RSA-2048 or ECDH and kept confidential for years is an HNDL target: recorded ciphertext can be decrypted once a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) exists; on timing, the Global Risk Institute's Quantum Threat Timeline Report 2025 (a survey of 26 experts) finds a "very significant chance" that a quantum computer able to factor RSA-2048 in under 24 hours is built within 10 years, with the experts divided, and that within 15 years "a clear majority considers a CRQC at least as likely as not". Data classification by HNDL risk must precede migration prioritization
- **Genomic Data Protection** — human genome sequences: ~3 GB per individual, typical study cohort 10,000–100,000 subjects; encryption at-rest with AES-256-GCM + ML-KEM-1024 KEK is the recommended pattern (AES-256 is quantum-resistant for confidentiality; ML-KEM replaces ECDH for key encapsulation); re-identification risk from genomic data makes it effectively permanent PII — the confidentiality lifetime extends to the subject's lifetime plus statutory retention periods; GA4GH (Global Alliance for Genomics and Health) Beacon v2 and Passports APIs authenticate with signed tokens; their algorithms will need to migrate when the ecosystem does
- **Academic Publishing and Preprint Integrity** — DOI-linked publications rely on digital signatures for long-term authenticity verification; preprint servers (arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN) do not currently sign submissions; co-author identity verification uses email-based or ORCID-based authentication — both need PQC upgrade paths; timestamp and non-repudiation proofs for priority claims (patent disputes, attribution) may need to stay verifiable for decades. Long-term verifiability is a lifecycle, not a single algorithm choice: a defined signature profile, binding of the signer's identity to a certificate, a trusted timestamp (RFC 3161), evidence records (RFC 4998), preservation of the certificates and revocation data needed for validation, and re-timestamping or archive renewal before the algorithms in use weaken. A stronger signature parameter set (for example NIST Level 5 ML-DSA or SLH-DSA) is one input to that plan, not the whole answer
- **HPC and Cloud Research Infrastructure** — HPC cluster authentication: Kerberos v5 + SSH host keys; ML-DSA SSH host key migration requires coordinated `known_hosts` updates across all submitter nodes; MPI job scheduler (SLURM, PBS Pro): TLS connections for job submission APIs; cloud burst computing (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud research programs): TLS and IAM credential exposure during burst periods extends HNDL surface; multi-institutional data sharing via Globus or rsync over SSH requires both endpoints to support PQC before the link is secure
- **Lattice-Based Cryptography Research** — ML-KEM rests on the Module Learning With Errors (Module-LWE) problem, and ML-DSA on Module-LWE and the Module Short Integer Solution (Module-SIS) problem — structured variants of LWE and SIS; current best lattice attacks (BKZ algorithm, core-SVP hardness) show no polynomial-time quantum speedup; researchers in lattice cryptanalysis, algebraic geometry, and number theory are the most relevant contributors to PQC security proofs; parameter selection in NIST finalists reflects conservative estimates of BKZ attack cost at quantum security levels (ML-KEM-768: NIST Category 3 ≈ AES-192 security)
- **Code-Based and Multivariate Cryptography** — code-based schemes (Classic McEliece, HQC, BIKE) rely on hardness of decoding random linear codes; McEliece has been studied since 1978 without a practical break of its recommended parameters, but on 1 Oct 2026 the BSI advised against it for new developments, citing 2026 progress in its cryptanalysis; large public key sizes (McEliece-8192128: 1 MB public key) limit adoption; multivariate polynomial systems (UOV, Rainbow, MAYO) offer small signatures but have had multiple breaks — Rainbow was broken in 2022; researchers should treat multivariate schemes as research-grade, not production-grade
- **Cross-Disciplinary PQC Applications** — biology + cryptography: DNA storage encryption (ML-KEM for key encapsulation over DNA-encoded data), genomic co-computation via FHE (Zama TFHE, Microsoft SEAL); physics + cryptography: QKD integration with PQC (hybrid QKD+ML-KEM for defense channels), quantum random number generation (QRNG) for entropy sourcing (ID Quantique, Toshiba QRNG); mathematics + cryptography: isogeny-based schemes (SIKE was broken in 2022; SQISign remains a research candidate); chemistry + cryptography: molecular simulation data protection (long-lived IP)
- **Quantum Random Number Generators (QRNG)** — SP 800-90A specifies DRBG mechanisms, SP 800-90B specifies entropy sources and how they are validated, and SP 800-90C specifies how the two are combined into RBG constructions; a QRNG is one kind of noise source inside an entropy source and needs the same SP 800-90B model, health tests and entropy assessment as any other. Certification is per product and per certificate: for example, CMVP Entropy Validation Certificate #E63 covers ID Quantique's Quantis IID QRNG chips (checked 2026-09-24); check the CMVP entropy-validation list for any other product before relying on a claim. Researchers studying entropy analysis should understand the SP 800-90B health tests (Repetition Count Test and Adaptive Proportion Test, §4.4)
- **Research Funding and PQC** — this module does not track funder-specific PQC requirements; check the current call text of the funder (for example NIH, NSF or Horizon Europe) before stating one. NSF's SaTC (Secure and Trustworthy Cyberspace) program funds cryptography research, including PQC. As advice rather than a funder requirement: researchers who hold long-lived sensitive data can describe PQC-ready data protection (data classification, crypto-agile storage, a migration plan) in the data management plan (DMP) of a proposal

## Workshop / Interactive Activities

The workshop has 4 steps:

1. **Why It Matters** — six research threat areas (long-lived research data, publication and peer-review integrity, research infrastructure, emerging PQC research, cross-disciplinary applications, funding and grant competitiveness), each with a severity, a timeframe and an example scenario
2. **What to Learn** — eight skill gaps (lattice-based cryptography, hash-based signatures, entropy and randomness, hybrid cryptography and formal methods, QKD, the PQC standards landscape, side-channel analysis, cross-disciplinary applications), each with a target level and links to related modules, grouped into four knowledge domains
3. **How to Act** — a phased action plan (immediate, 30-day, 90-day, 6-month) with checklists, quick wins and KPI targets
4. **Research Readiness Self-Assessment** — a nine-item weighted checklist (data needing >10-year confidentiality, HPC or shared infrastructure access over SSH/VPN, multi-institution data sharing, digitally signed publications or code, cryptography-related research, grant proposals with security sections, limited PQC awareness, patentable or commercially sensitive IP, no institutional data classification policy for quantum risk) that produces an exposure score with a low, moderate or high band and a short recommendation

## Related Standards

- FIPS 203 (ML-KEM — Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism)
- FIPS 204 (ML-DSA — Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Standard)
- FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA — Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Standard)
- NIST SP 800-208 (Recommendation for Stateful Hash-Based Signature Schemes — LMS and XMSS)
- NIST SP 800-90A Rev. 1 (Recommendation for Random Number Generation Using DRBGs)
- NIST SP 800-90B (Recommendation for the Entropy Sources Used for Random Bit Generation)
- NIST SP 800-90C (Recommendation for Random Bit Generator Constructions — final, published September 2025)
- NIST IR 8309 (Status Report on the Second Round of the NIST PQC Standardization Process — July 2020)
- NIST IR 8413 (Status Report on the Third Round of the NIST PQC Standardization Process — July 2022; advanced BIKE, Classic McEliece, HQC, and SIKE to the fourth round)
- NIST IR 8105 (Report on Post-Quantum Cryptography — 2016 original assessment)
- NIH Genomic Data Sharing Policy (2014, updated 2023 — data retention and access controls)
- FDA 21 CFR Part 11 (Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — retention periods come from the applicable predicate rule, e.g. 21 CFR 312.62(c))
- EU Regulation 536/2014 on clinical trials (Art. 58 — clinical trial master file archived for at least 25 years)
- Global Risk Institute, Quantum Threat Timeline Report 2025 (expert survey on CRQC timing)
- GA4GH Framework for Responsible Sharing of Genomic and Health-Related Data
