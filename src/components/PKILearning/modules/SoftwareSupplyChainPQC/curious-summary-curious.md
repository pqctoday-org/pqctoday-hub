Much of the software you install arrives with a signature, a bit of maths that lets your device check who sent it and that nobody changed it on the way. That check is only as good as the rules behind it: who is allowed to sign, how many keys must agree, and what the device does when something looks wrong.

The documents that set those rules are not all at the same point. Some already describe signatures built to resist quantum computers, some are still drafts, and some do not mention the subject at all. That last one is a fact about the document, not a statement about what anyone plans to do.

So the useful question is not "is it safe?" but "which part of the chain does this document cover, and what does it actually say?"
