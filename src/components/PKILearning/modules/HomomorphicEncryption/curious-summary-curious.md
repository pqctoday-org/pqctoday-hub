Imagine handing someone a locked box with a glove-port, so they can work on what is inside without ever being able to open the lid. That is homomorphic encryption: a cloud service can add up, compare or analyse your data while it stays locked, and hands the result back still locked. Only you hold the key.

The catch is that the key is everything. Anyone allowed to ask the key-holder to open boxes could piece the key together from the answers. So the key is best kept in a dedicated security device that only opens a box when the rules say so, and the rules on who may ask matter as much as the device. The glove-port also makes the work slow, so it is used where keeping the data locked is worth the wait.

The mathematics behind it is the same family used in the new quantum-safe standards, and no one knows how to break it with a quantum computer. The parts around it, such as how the keys are sent, still need to be upgraded.
